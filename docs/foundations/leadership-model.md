# Leadership Model

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Version:** 1
- **Owner:** Brad
- **Last updated:** 2026-07-25

This document describes how Brad Files leads, the responsibilities he is
stewarding, the rhythms and conditions that support his best work, and the
areas in which coaching is most valuable. It personalizes the
[Constitution](constitution.md) for Brad in service of the
[Product Vision](../product/vision.md).

The Leadership Model is descriptive rather than constitutional. It informs how
Chief of Staff should adapt its recommendations, but it does not override the
Vision or Constitution and does not prescribe software behavior. Brad's
explicit current instruction governs present execution within those boundaries
and may supersede this descriptive model when his circumstances change.

Include only the minimum operationally necessary personal context and follow
the repository's
[privacy and sensitivity policy](../../AGENTS.md#privacy-and-sensitivity).

## Leadership Horizons

Brad leads across several horizons at once. Healthy recommendations should
connect today's work to the longer responsibilities it serves.

| Horizon | Leadership focus |
| --- | --- |
| **Today** | Execute today's commitments faithfully. |
| **This Week** | Deliver recurring ministry rhythms, including scripts, video production, follow-up, and meetings. |
| **This Season** | Use a rolling, approximately 90-day execution horizon to move strategic initiatives and major projects forward. |
| **Six Months** | Advance Brad's official ministry goals, organizational commitments, and performance goals. |
| **Calling** | Grow as a disciple, shepherd, husband, father, leader, and builder of lasting ministries. |

The six-month horizon has special importance because it represents formally
agreed goals and commitments. Seasonal execution should advance those goals
rather than displace them.

## Primary Stewardships

Brad's responsibilities should be evaluated as a portfolio rather than as
competing task lists.

- **Spiritual health:** Sustain the prayer, Scripture, worship, and reflection
  from which faithful leadership flows.
- **Family:** Remain present and faithful as a husband and father; family is a
  first-class stewardship, not residual capacity.
- **Online Campus leadership:** Provide pastoral, strategic, and operational
  leadership for Online Campus and its ministry.
- **Team building and leader development:** Develop people, clarify ownership,
  and multiply leadership rather than becoming the center of every decision.
- **Innovation and systems improvement:** Create better tools, processes, and
  ministry systems that produce lasting value.
- **Organizational responsibilities:** Honor commitments to Northridge Church,
  ministry partners, leadership, and the wider organization.
- **Personal health:** Protect the physical and emotional capacity required for
  sustainable leadership.
- **Lifelong learning:** Continue growing through reading, reflection,
  experimentation, feedback, and exposure to ideas across disciplines.

## Protected Rhythms

These rhythms are defaults to protect, not rigid rules. Ministry, family, or
genuine human needs may require flexibility, but exceptions should not quietly
become the normal pattern.

- Daily prayer and Scripture.
- Deep work during morning hours whenever possible.
- A monthly Prayer & Planning Day.
- Chapel House retreats in January and July.
- A weekly planning and review rhythm.
- Friday as a flexible Sabbath and day off.
- Margin before major ministry events.

## Energy Profile

- Brad's highest cognitive energy is generally in the morning.
- Energy commonly declines in mid-afternoon.
- Creative work is strongest when it precedes execution and administrative
  work.
- Deep work is most productive before meetings whenever possible.
- A variety of environments—including the office, a coffee shop, a library,
  or a retreat setting—improves thinking.
- Quiet environments remain important for concentration despite Brad being
  highly relational.

These patterns should guide planning without becoming assumptions that
override Brad's current energy, circumstances, or explicit direction.

## Working Style

Brad's natural strengths include wisdom, innovation, creativity, and
interdisciplinary thinking. He tends to see broad possibilities and
connections before arranging work into a sequential plan.

Recurring challenges include follow-through, honest estimation,
overcommitment, and procrastination. Brad benefits from external organization,
clear prioritization, visible tradeoffs, and encouragement to finish meaningful
work before expanding into new work.

He is naturally drawn to creating systems and improving how work is done.
Recommendations should help him invest that strength where it creates durable
value while ensuring repetitive execution is appropriately owned and
completed.

## Delegation Model

- Delegate when another person can perform the work at approximately 80%
  effectiveness.
- Invest in defining the outcome, coaching, and encouragement rather than
  reclaiming delegated work prematurely.
- Build leaders instead of allowing Brad to become the bottleneck.
- Protect the work that Brad can uniquely do.
- Measure successful delegation by increasing team capability, ownership, and
  confidence over time.

Delegation should remain attentive to risk, readiness, pastoral sensitivity,
and the legitimate interests of the people affected.

## Success Measures

Success should be considered through meaningful, transparent indicators rather
than hidden or reductive scoring, consistent with the Constitution.

- Commitments are reliably honored.
- Teammates trust timelines and expectations.
- Fewer responsibilities are dropped or quietly forgotten.
- Planning extends further beyond the immediate horizon.
- More time is available for deep work.
- Leadership and ownership are multiplied.
- Ministry becomes healthier and more sustainable.
- 360-degree feedback shows improvement in planning, follow-through, and
  priority management.
- Brad experiences more peace and less cognitive overload.
- Brad continues becoming more faithful to his personal calling and
  Northridge's mission.

## Decision Filters

Use these questions to evaluate competing opportunities, requests, and
commitments:

1. Is this part of Brad's primary stewardship?
2. Does this advance Northridge's mission?
3. Is this important or merely urgent?
4. Is Brad the right person to do this?
5. Can this be delegated?
6. Does this deserve Brad's highest-energy hours?
7. What is the opportunity cost of saying yes?
8. Does this strengthen people, systems, or both?
9. What preparation should happen before execution?
10. If this were the only thing accomplished today, would it have been a good
    day?

These filters support judgment; they do not replace prayer, pastoral
discernment, or Brad's decision.
